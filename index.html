Your pet isn't a piece of luggage. They're family.

You know the feeling. You're planning a trip to France, Spain, Portugal — somewhere warm, somewhere beautiful. And the very first thought isn't where to stay or what to eat. It's: "Can we bring the cats?"

For millions of UK pet owners, leaving their dog or cat behind isn't really an option. Not for a week. Not even for a long weekend. These are animals that sleep on your bed, sit on your lap, follow you from room to room. The idea of kennels or a cat sitter fills you with guilt before you've even packed a bag.

So you decide to bring them. Obviously. Of course you do.

Then you discover the paperwork.

Before April 2022, UK pet owners had EU pet passports. One document, valid for life, that let you take your pet across the channel whenever you liked. Simple.

Then Brexit happened. Then the rules changed again. And again. And in April 2026, the last loophole closed — EU-issued pet passports no longer work for GB residents at all.

Now, every single trip to Europe requires an Animal Health Certificate (AHC) — a document that:

  • Can only be issued by a specially qualified Official Veterinarian (OV)
  • Costs between £100 and £300 every time
  • Is only valid for 10 days from the date of issue
  • Covers just one entry into the EU
  • Requires your rabies vaccine to be administered at least 21 days before travel
  • Requires the microchip to be implanted before the rabies jab — not after
A family taking two cats to Spain and back costs £400–£800 in documentation alone — every trip.

And that's before flights, accommodation, or a single glass of sangria.

And the stakes are brutally high.

Get one number wrong on the AHC — a single digit transposed on your pet's microchip — and your animal is refused entry at the border. Not delayed. Not held for checking. Refused. You either go back to the UK, or your pet goes into quarantine at your expense while you figure out what to do.

It has happened. It happens regularly. And it almost always happens to people who thought they'd done everything right.

How do UK pet owners get to Europe?

You have more options than you might think — not just flying.

🚂

Eurotunnel Le Shuttle

Pets travel in the car with you. Most popular route for dog and cat owners. Book pet space in advance.

⛴️

Ferry

DFDS and Brittany Ferries both allow pets. Some routes have pet-friendly cabins. Book early in summer.

✈️

Flying

Small pets (under 8–10kg) can travel in-cabin on some airlines. Larger pets travel as cargo — rules vary by airline.

🚗

Drive via Chunnel

Load the car onto the train at Folkestone. 35 minutes to Calais. Smoothest experience for anxious pets.

Whichever route you choose, the AHC requirement is the same. The checklist covers all travel methods.

Getting this wrong is expensive

🚫

Turned away at the border

If your microchip number doesn't match your AHC exactly, your pet cannot enter the EU. Full stop.

💰

Wasted vet appointments

An AHC costs £100–£300. Show up without the right documents and you'll pay for a second visit.

⏱️

The 21-day clock starts now

Your rabies vaccine must be given at least 21 days before travel. Book your vet today — not next week.

📋

EU passports no longer valid

April 2026 changed everything. Even if your pet had a passport before Brexit, it no longer works for UK residents travelling to the EU.
