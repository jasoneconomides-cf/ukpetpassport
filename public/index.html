You've got a trip to Europe on the cards. Maybe it's booked. Maybe it's still a dream. Either way, before you've looked at routes, the first question — the only question — is: "Can we bring them?"

Not should we. Can we. The idea of a week in kennels — the guilt alone would ruin the trip. They're family. Of course they're coming.

"The hotel is booked. The route is planned. Now how do we get the dog — and the cats — across the channel?"

Then you find out what Brexit did to pet travel.

In April 2026, the last door closed: EU-issued pet passports no longer work for GB residents.

Now, every single trip requires an Animal Health Certificate (AHC):

  • Issued by a qualified Official Veterinarian (OV)
  • Costs £100 to £300 every single trip
  • Valid for just 10 days
  • One entry into the EU — new one needed every time
  • Rabies vaccine at least 21 days before travel
  • Microchip must be implanted before the rabies jab
One dog + one cat. A trip to Spain and back.
£400–£800 in paperwork — every single time.

Before you've spent a penny on accommodation, food, or fuel.

And one small mistake turns it into a nightmare.

One transposed digit on the microchip number. An AHC issued 11 days before travel instead of 10. Any one of those — and your pet is refused entry at the border.

The only thing standing between a perfect holiday and a border catastrophe is knowing the checklist cold — before you leave the driveway.
