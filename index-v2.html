You've got a trip to Europe coming up. Or you're thinking about one. And before you've even looked at flights or ferries, the first question — the only question that actually matters — is: "Can we take them with us?"

Not "should we." Can we. Because leaving them behind isn't really on the table. A week in a cattery, a weekend at the kennels — the guilt alone would ruin the trip. These are animals that sleep on your pillow, sit on your laptop, follow you from room to room like a furry shadow. They're family. Of course they're coming.

"The hotel is booked. The route is planned. Now how do we get the cats across the channel?"

Then you discover what Brexit actually did to pet travel.

For a while, it wasn't that bad. EU pet passports still worked. One document, valid for life, and your dog or cat could cross the channel whenever you liked.

Then the rules changed. Then they changed again. And in April 2026, the last door closed: EU-issued pet passports no longer work for GB residents at all.

Now, every single trip to Europe — every holiday, every visit to family, every long weekend in Brittany — requires an Animal Health Certificate (AHC). A document that:

  • Can only be issued by a specially qualified Official Veterinarian (OV) — not your regular vet
  • Costs between £100 and £300 every single time
  • Is only valid for 10 days from the date it's issued
  • Covers just one entry into the EU — you need a new one for every trip
  • Requires a rabies vaccination given at least 21 days before travel
  • Requires the microchip to be implanted before the rabies jab, not after
Two cats. One trip to Spain and back. £400–£800 in paperwork costs alone.

Every year. Every trip. Before you've spent a penny on accommodation.

And one mistake turns it into a nightmare.

A single transposed digit on your pet's microchip number. A rabies vaccine recorded before the microchip implant. An AHC issued 11 days before travel instead of 10.

Any one of these — and your pet is refused entry at the border. Not held for inspection. Not delayed. Refused. You either turn around and go home, or your pet goes into quarantine at your cost while you figure out what happens next.

This isn't a worst-case scenario. It happens. Regularly. To people who thought they'd done everything right.

The only thing standing between a perfect holiday and a border catastrophe is knowing the checklist cold — before you leave the driveway.

That's why we built the UK Pet Passport Checklist 2026. It won't make the AHC cheaper or fix the politics. But it will make sure you arrive at the Eurotunnel with everything right — every document, every date, every number — so the only thing you're thinking about is what to have for dinner in Normandy.

How do UK pet owners get to Europe?

More options than you might think — and most of them are easier than flying.

🚂

Eurotunnel Le Shuttle

Your pet stays in the car with you. Most popular for dog and cat owners. Book pet space in advance.

⛴️

Ferry

DFDS and Brittany Ferries allow pets. Some routes have pet-friendly cabins. Book early in summer.

✈️

Flying

Small pets (under 8–10kg) can travel in-cabin on some airlines. Larger pets travel as cargo — rules vary.

🚗

Drive via Chunnel

Load the car onto the train at Folkestone. 35 minutes to Calais. Smoothest for anxious pets.
